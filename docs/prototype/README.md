# Steelman prototype

Open **index.html** in a modern browser. It starts on the list of steelmen; choose “Is open-weight AI a smart bet for China?” for the full example board. No installation or server is required. An internet connection loads the fonts and supporting browser assets.

Argument cards use a grayscale paper texture, rounded corners, and soft shadows; the question and option headers are inverted (near-black boxes, white serif). Each card is judged on three axes:

- **Credibility:** a four-segment support meter in the card's footer. Filled segments are the share of assessments that find the wording supported, colored red (unsupported) → gray (contested) → green (supported). Dashed empty segments mean fewer than five assessments; an empty gray meter means unreviewed.
- **Importance:** the vertical vote beside each row, with an axis, in a white pill, down the edge of the first visible column.
- **Articulation:** the horizontal vote above each version, right under the axis of an unfolded row. An argument with only one wording has no articulation vote: there is nothing to compare it with yet.

Importance and articulation share one vote control, turned a quarter turn, and their axes use map-style halos. After a vote, a word ("Important.", "Less important.", "Well articulated.", "Weaker articulation.") appears beside the arrow and fades, repeating the axis direction. Behind each column, a faint texture of filled hexagons carries the same two votes: importance sets how many cells are present (a full honeycomb behind the most important row, a scattering behind the least), and tone fades across an unfolded row from the best-articulated version.

Colors, type, and texture come from [`../design/tokens.css`](../design/tokens.css). After editing the source or the tokens, run `python3 scripts/sync-prototype.py` from the repository root to update the standalone export; the script copies the tokens in, because the sandboxed prototype cannot load local files. The original supplied prototype remains in Git history.

## Around the board: spaces, sharing, presence, and creating

The prototype now opens on a list of steelmen, a mock of how people find, share, and start them. Think Reddit for the list and Figma for the board. Everything is simulated and local to your browser.

- **Spaces.** The dark sidebar lists your private spaces (POD 6, Northwind Studio, Personal) and the public communities you've joined (Brooklyn Streets, Campus Life, Future of Work). Home, your spaces, and your communities each sit in their own quiet box. Every space is named the same way: its name in capitals, in a rounded box. Each steelman lives in one space, which sets who can find it and gives its board a canvas, a pale tint and texture (POD 6 warm stone with contours, Northwind cool gray with dots, Personal sand, Brooklyn slate with contours, Campus Life sage with linen, Future of Work lilac with dots). The same canvas shows on the board's miniature in the list and as a swatch beside the space in the sidebar. On wide screens a right-hand column shows **Needs your help** (boards with a thin option) and **Your drafts** on Home, or the space's own card (canvas banner, about, members, **Join / Joined**) on a space; narrower, the space card sits above the list. Leaving a community removes it from the sidebar. Below 900 px the sidebar folds behind the ☰ button in the header.
- **The list.** Each steelman shows an activity score, its space, author, and age, the question, each option with its argument count, the comment count, who is there now, and a miniature of the board. The score is not a separate up/down vote: it adds up the board's importance votes, articulation votes, evidence assessments, and comments (hover it for the breakdown). A draft in Personal shows "Draft" instead. An option with fewer than two arguments has a dashed edge, the board's sign for "add to this". Options appear as small dark headers in serif, like the option headers on the board. Sort by **Hot**, **New**, **Top**, or **Needs help** (boards where an option has fewer than two arguments). Search filters by question or option. Click anywhere on an item to open it.
- **Opening and leaving a board.** The board grows out of its miniature; the breadcrumb (**steelman. / POD 6**), the wordmark, or Escape shrinks it back into the list.
- **Keyboard and screen readers.** Every control is a button with a name; tooltips open on focus and close with Escape; status messages are announced. A board starts with **Jump to** links (hidden until focused) that skip to each side, since each argument has about ten stops. Reduced-motion settings stop all movement. The muted grey text tokens were darkened so every label reaches 4.5:1 on white and on every space canvas.
- **On phones** (640 px and narrower), a board shows one side at a time. Tabs under the question name each side with its argument count; they pin to the top of the screen as you scroll, and switching keeps your place on each side. Swiping stays with unfolded rows, which scroll sideways, so a swipe never means two things. Opening something on the other side (a link, a notification, the tour, review) switches to it. Strongest case still shows both sides, stacked.
- **Links.** Every board, version, and comment has an address, and the page's address follows what you have open: `…/prototype/#b=openweights` for a board, `&v=independenceNarrow` for an open version, `#b=openweights&c=c-y1` for a comment. Back and Forward move between the list and boards; reload returns to the same place. **Copy link** is in every version's ⋯ menu, and **Link** under every comment. Opening a link brings the version into view whatever hides it (a hidden column, a fold, its row's stack), opens its context and evidence, and rings it; a comment link opens its thread and rings the comment. A version's link survives combining and separating (its id doesn't change). A deleted version or comment opens the board with "That version was deleted. · Dismiss"; an unknown board shows "This steelman doesn't exist". Signed out, a link to a board that isn't open to anyone with the link asks you to sign in ("Sign in to open this steelman", without showing the question), then lands where it pointed.
- **Board and Strongest case.** The switch in the top bar moves between **Board**, everything, and **Strongest case**: each option's three most important arguments (shared Top order), each in its best-articulated wording, with its evidence meter, importance, and how many wordings it beat. A side with fewer than two arguments says it needs a stronger case. Click an argument to open it on the board. With nothing open, Escape steps back: Strongest case → Board → the list.
- **Who's here.** The classmates who have a board open appear as faces in the top bar (hover for names). Nothing about them appears on the cards.
- **Share.** The **Share** button opens a panel: invite by username (suggested from the space) or by email, with a role: **can view** (read and comment), **can vote** (also vote and assess evidence), or **can contribute** (also write). People with access lists the owner and everyone invited, with role menus and remove. **General access** is only people invited, everyone in the space, or anyone with the link, with its own role; the pill under the question follows it. Only the owner can change access; on someone else's board you can still invite and copy the link. **Copy link** copies a working link that opens the invite landing (below). Settings keeps the question and its space and links to Share.
- **Roles and Preview as.** Each role can do only what Share says: viewers read and comment, voters also vote and assess evidence, contributors also write, and only the owner opens Settings. Controls you can't use fade and say why when hovered or clicked; prompts to write in a row and **+ Option** disappear. The board header says what you can do, with **Request access** (simulated) when you can't write, or **Sign in** when signed out. You can't invite anyone above your own access. **Preview as** in the top bar is a prototype tool, not a product feature: it switches between your access and Owner, Contributor, Voter, Viewer, and Signed out, so testers can try each role. The plan for the next steps is in `plans/permissions.md`.
- **AI suggestions (ochre robot).** An ochre robot always means "AI did this"; hover it (tap on touch screens) for the explanation. The open-weights board shows AI checks switched on, so every card has one, right after the people's meter and never part of it. The dot beside the robot uses the meter's colors and fill: green and full = AI thinks the evidence supports the wording, gray and half = partly, red and empty = doesn't, dashed = no sources to check. Open the influence argument and a robot on each source says what AI made of it, and one beside "Does the evidence back this wording?" gives its limits. One argument, at the bottom of "Why it makes sense", is an **AI draft**: dotted ochre border and an "AI draft" tag (hover for its basis and limits); it starts like any new argument (no votes, unreviewed, no sources). On it, the ⋯ menu offers **Write a better version**; the owner can also combine it or **Remove this AI draft** (with Undo). **Checks run themselves**: with Check evidence on (How it runs), a new argument or wording, an accepted one, and any change to a card's evidence (adding, editing, or removing a source, or reporting one unavailable) start a check: the robot pulses for a moment, then shows its verdict, and the space spends one credit. In an opened card, the small ochre button beside the evidence question checks it (again) on request; anyone who can write can use it. When AI can't open a source (one with no "where to find it", or one already unavailable), its row offers **Mark unavailable**, which a person clicks. All AI output is simulated: no model runs, and verdicts on new evidence are placeholders. The plan is in `plans/ai-integrations.md`.
- **How it runs.** Below the board skeleton in **New steelman**, two quiet rows: **Space** (with who can see it) and **How it runs**, which summarizes the board's rules ("Open team · AI checks") and opens to set them; the same controls are in the board's Settings. They follow the Share panel's style: small tracked headings (Mode, Votes, AI, Timing), rows split by hairlines, the control on the right. Mode is three tiles (the chosen one outlined in ink), editors are initials, votes are outlined checks (dashed when off), AI features are switches with their cost under the name, and the closing date starts as **Add a date**. **Mode** is a preset: **Open team** (new arguments publish right away; anyone who can write reorganizes), **Edited** (editors review new arguments and reorganize), or **Public** (publish right away; editors reorganize). Changing either setting by hand makes it **Custom rules**. Pick **Editors** from the space's people when a setting needs them. **Votes** switches importance, articulation, and evidence on or off for the board; switched-off votes disappear from it. **AI** switches on drafting, evidence checks, and duplicate flags, with each one's cost and the space's credits; features a space doesn't allow are faded (Brooklyn Streets allows only checks; Campus Life allows none). **Draft arguments** fills each option of the new board with a simulated AI draft and spends 20 credits. **Closes** sets an optional date. The board header shows the mode (and closing date) as a pill; for the owner it opens Settings. Reorganizing is enforced: when it's set to Editors or Only the owner, others can't add, rename, or delete options and suggest combinations instead. A board closes on its date, or when the owner chooses **Close now** in Settings (**Reopen** undoes it).
- **Editors and review.** Share has a fourth role, **can edit**: everything a contributor can do, plus reviewing and reorganizing. On a board, the editors in How it runs are the people Share lists as "can edit". With **New arguments: Editors review first**, an argument or a new wording from anyone below editor is held: a faint dashed card tagged **Awaiting review** (or **New wording · awaiting review**, under its row), seen only by its author and the editors. Editors **Accept** it (it joins the board with its author) or **Decline** it with an optional reason; the author can **Withdraw** it, and sees a declined one, with the reason, until they **Dismiss** it. Editors get a **2 to review** button in the top bar that steps through held items in screen order. The capstone example ("Should capstone teams be allowed to ship AI-written code?") runs Edited, with you as an editor and two items waiting. **Preview as** now includes **Editor**.
- **Moderation.** Anyone signed in can **Report** a version (its ⋯ menu) or a comment, as Spam, Abusive, Off-topic, or Something else, and withdraw the report. Reports are seen only by editors, who act as moderators on a public board: a reported version shows a red-edged flag with its count on the card's edge (hover for the reasons), and reported versions join the **to review** stepper. Moderators can **Hide** a version or comment with a reason: it keeps its place as "Removed by a moderator · Spam", with the original text struck through for moderators only, and **Restore** brings it back. Removed arguments drop out of Strongest case. The clock in the question header opens **History**: who hid, restored, dismissed reports on, accepted, declined, or deleted what, with the reason and when (reports themselves stay out of it, to protect reporters). The bike-lane example in Brooklyn Streets is a public board you moderate: one spam argument reported twice, one removed for abuse, and a reported comment.
- **Closing and the decision.** A closed board is read only: votes, writing, review, and reorganizing stop ("Closed." on hover); comments stay open. The header pill says "Closed Nov 7", and the list item shows "Decided: Go async" (or "Closed"). Inside the dark question box, the owner records the **Decision**: the chosen option, why, and the arguments that mattered, picked from each side's top three. Everyone else sees it there; each cited argument opens its card. Recording, closing, and reopening go in History. Settings also lists **Who's weighed in**: everyone with access, marked Wrote, Voted, or Not yet (simulated from authors and a sample of voters). The Northwind standups example is closed with priya_s's decision. Reopening a board clears its decision (the History entry stays).
- **Invite landing and the tour.** **Copy link** in Share now copies a working link to this prototype (`…/prototype/#s=<board>&by=<you>&as=<role>`). Opening it, or **Preview** beside Copy link, shows what an invited person sees first: the board's canvas, who invited them, the question and its options as the board's own dark headers and wires, what their role lets them do, and how many people are there. Signing in has no password: **Continue with Google**, or **Email me a link**. Both confirm the email, so there are no unconfirmed accounts. After a link is sent, a **Simulate** box offers **Open the link** and **Open it too late** (which shows "That link has expired" and **Email me a new link**); **Resend** and **Use a different email** sit below. A malformed email says "That doesn't look like an email address." Someone new (Google from an invite, or any email other than yours) then chooses a username: taken names (jordan_w) and anything but 3 to 20 letters, numbers, dots, or underscores are refused. Your own email (kieran_lamee@example.com, or whatever you changed it to) signs straight in. You always continue as kieran_lamee, and Preview as switches to the invited role. **Look around first** opens the board signed out. **Sign in** in the top bar, or under a board's question, opens the same panel ("Sign in to take part", with the board's question); it returns you to that board, and **Not now** goes back signed out. After signing in, a first visit starts a short tour on the board itself: the page dims around real cards, with a short note for each vote, taken from the board. Importance shows the side's top argument; Articulation unfolds the argument with the most wordings; Evidence shows the most-assessed wording and its share supported. Votes a board has switched off are skipped. **Next**, **Back**, **Skip tour** (or Escape), and arrow keys move through it. A real link tours only a first visit; Preview always shows the whole thing. **Tour** in your profile's Account card takes it again on the board you came from. On phones the tour frames just the leading wording.
- **Notifications.** A bell beside your avatar shows how many are unread; it opens an inbox below the top bar (hidden when signed out). Each notification says who did what, quotes the wording or comment when there is one, and names the board (with its canvas swatch) and when. Opening one marks it read and takes you to its place: a reply opens the comment thread; an accepted item rings its card; a declined one rings the held card with its reason; "waiting for your review" and "reports to look at" step to the first item, like the to-review button; a decision scrolls to it; a join request opens the space's settings at **Approve**. The dot on the right marks one read or unread, and **Mark all read** clears them. Escape or a click outside closes it. Some notifications are worked out from the boards as they stand, so they stay true: items to review or moderate on boards you edit (they come back when the count changes), boards closing today or tomorrow, decisions on boards you took part in, and requests to join private spaces you admin. The example adds a reply to your comment on the "China controls AI" card, a declined wording on the capstone board (which now closes Nov 10), an invitation to edit it, and rtorres accepting your bike-lane argument. During a visit, accepting or declining something you submitted (try it with Preview as) adds one. On phones, the wordmark shows only its logo, and a board's top bar drops the breadcrumb and your avatar to make room.
- **Creating and running a space.** **+ New space** at the bottom of the sidebar opens a page with the space's canvas as a banner: name it, add a line about it, pick one of the six canvases, and choose **Private space** or **Public community**. **Create space** adds it to the sidebar (a community is joined automatically) and to the New steelman space picker, makes you its admin, gives it a 100-credit monthly AI budget with evidence checks on, and opens its settings at the invite field. On any space you admin, the space card has **Settings** (Preview as other than Your access or Owner hides it). Changes save as you make them:
  - **Basics:** name, about, canvas, and who can join; the banner and sidebar follow along.
  - **Members:** requests to join a private space come first, with **Approve** and **Decline** (POD 6 has two). Invite by username or email with a role (simulated: nothing is sent); invites wait in the list until cancelled. Everyone has a role menu (**Admin**, **Moderator**, **Member**) and a remove ×. The last admin can't step down, and stepping down yourself closes Settings.
  - **Rules:** up to five short rules, numbered, edited in place. The space card lists them, followed by its **Moderators** (admins and moderators, each opening their profile). Admins see no Join or Leave on their own community.
  - **AI** (finishes plan A1): which AI features boards in the space may use (switching one off fades it in every board's How it runs), the **monthly budget**, how much is used this month (with a bar), what's left, and recent usage.
  - **New steelmen start as:** the mode How it runs starts with for boards created in this space.
  - Personal has only Basics (no access choice), AI, and the starting mode.
- **Profiles and points.** The avatar in the top bar opens your profile, and any username (in the list, under the question, on a card, in comments or History) opens that person's. A dark header shows their initials, name, spaces (each opens the space), and **points**. Points are like karma, with no money: they reward strengthening the debate whichever side it's on. An argument in a side's top three (on a side with at least four) earns 25, a wording that leads a row with others in it earns 10, a submission an editor accepts earns 15, and a source cited by a wording that more assessments find supported than not earns 5. The **Points** card breaks the total down; points appear only on profiles, never on cards. **Contributions** lists their arguments, wordings, and sources across boards, newest first, with what each earned; each opens its card on the board. Your own profile adds **Credits** (Personal and the spaces you admin, POD 6, with recent AI usage, each opening its board) and **Account**: username; email with **Change**, which sends a link to the new address and shows it as **Confirming** (with **Resend**, **Cancel**, and a simulated **Open the link**) until it's opened; **Sign in with** Google (**Connect** / **Disconnect**) and Email link (always on); the tour; **Privacy**: **Usage analytics** (On/Off, the same real setting as the notice; "Off · browser signal" when the browser asks not to be tracked), **Your data** (**Download** saves steelman-data.json: your account, votes, and what you wrote and commented on each board, from this browser), and **Privacy notice**; **Sign out**, which switches Preview as to Signed out; and **Delete account**: it lists what's removed (email, votes, assessments, comments) and what stays, credited to deleted_account (arguments, wordings, sources), and asks you to type your username. Deleting is simulated: it shows "Your account is deleted", then signs you out. Back, or Escape, returns to where you came from. Example users carry points from earlier boards.
- **New steelman.** **+ New steelman** in the header opens a sheet laid out like the board's skeleton: a dark question box and two to four dark option headers, then the space (defaulting to the one you're viewing) and what that means for who can see it. Gentle hints appear for loaded words, "Why…" questions, and options named Yes or No. A question, at least two options, and different option names are required. Escape or Cancel keeps the draft. Creating zooms into the new, empty board, ready for the first argument; back in the list it starts with your upvote.

The usernames, spaces, list details, and other boards are illustrative. The open-weights board is the fully worked example: “Why it makes sense” and “Why it might not”. Its sources point to public documents and reporting (OpenAI cutting off API access from China, restrictions on DeepSeek’s app, US clouds hosting DeepSeek-R1, China’s rules and filings for public AI services, the US government evaluation of DeepSeek, and others); its votes and assessments are illustrative.

## Try it on the open-weights board

- Click the paper stack to the right of a card. Its dark badge is how many wordings there are; **≫** means there is no other wording yet, and opens a draft for one. The versions unfold in place, in the column's sort order (best articulated first under Top); the column widens, the row scrolls sideways if needed, and the wires stay attached.
- **The order never changes under you.** Arguments sit by shared importance and wordings by shared articulation, for everyone; your own votes don't move cards. The order you see is fixed when the board opens. As other people vote, add, and delete, a dark pill at the bottom of the screen counts the changes ("3 changes · **Update**"). **Update** (or reloading) applies them: rows slide to their new places while the row you're reading (the open card, or the first row on screen) stays where it was.
- **Other people, live (simulated).** **Simulate others** in the bottom bar is a prototype tool, like Preview as. **Votes** moves one row in each column toward the top and a wording ahead of its row's leader; counts update in place with a brief ring, but nothing moves until you update. **New argument** and **New wording** arrive at the end of their column or row (the open row, if any), so they never push anything. The **Open card** tools act on the expanded card, or the open row's first wording. **Edit** (someone else's version only) changes its wording in place; its byline then says "edited", which shows the earlier wording, and its credibility assessments start over. **Delete** marks it "Deleted by jordan_w" (or "Removed by an editor") in place. **Combine** shrinks the whole argument to one line, "lin.zhao combined this with '…'", with **Go to it**. **Separate** (a row with two or more wordings) marks a wording "omar_h made this a separate argument". Each keeps its place until Update, which then removes the deleted version, moves the combined wordings to the end of the other argument's row, and puts the separated wording at the end of the column as its own argument. A draft you were writing goes with the argument it belonged to; if that argument is gone, it becomes a new argument in the same column. **Offline** shows "Offline · 2 waiting to send" while your own actions queue (nothing from others arrives), then "Back online · 2 sent".
- **Highlight.** The highlighter in each option header marks wordings worth a look without moving anything: **New** (added in the last two days) or **Hot** (the three wordings gaining votes fastest). Matching cards get a small dark tag; rows without a match fade until hovered; a folded row with a match in its stack says "1 new wording inside" and rings its badge. A bar under the header counts the matches, and its ↑ ↓ buttons move your view to each in turn: they open whatever hides it (the column's fold, the row's stack, the row's own fold), scroll down and sideways to it, and ring it. Unfolding a row with a highlight on opens it on its first match.
- **At scale.** A column shows its 10 most important arguments; the rest fold into a stack ("190 more arguments · less important"), which shows 20 more at a time, with **Fold back to 10** after. An unfolded row shows its 5 best wordings, then a "+7 more wordings" card. Both folds say how many highlighted wordings they hold. Whatever is open, being edited, or stepped to is always shown, and pick mode for combining shows every row.
- **Hide columns.** An option's **⋯** menu has **Hide this column** and **Show only this column**. A hidden column becomes a narrow dark strip with its name and argument count; click it to bring the column back. At least one column stays visible.
- Vote on articulation with the arrows above each version, and on importance with the arrows beside the row.
- Each version shows who wrote it, small and light under the wording ("you" for yours). AI drafts say so in their tag instead.
- Click a card's wording or its chevron to reveal context and evidence; click again to collapse. The meter and source count in the footer are information only. A card with no comments shows its comment button faintly, and fully on hover.
- Click the dashed card at the end of a row to write a better version directly in the row. A dashed paper edge means an argument has only one version so far.
- Fold the row with the button at the end of the articulation axis, or press Escape (Escape first closes a menu, pick mode, a reply, the side panel, a source form, an edit, a draft argument or version, or an expanded card).
- Click **Add an argument** at the bottom of a column to write a new argument in place, with optional context. It joins the bottom of that column as its own row: importance 0, unreviewed, no sources, and a dashed stack edge inviting other versions. Escape or × keeps the draft for later.
- **Combining arguments that make the same point.** Drag the merge icon in a card's footer onto another argument in the same option (or click the icon to pick one; touch screens use the click). Dropping a wording from a row with several onto the column's **Add an argument** slot makes it a separate argument. Votes and comments travel with a moved version; a row left empty disappears; an Undo bar appears for ten seconds. Who can do it:
  - The **board owner** and a version's **author** combine or separate it directly.
  - Other people who **can contribute** drag to *suggest* it. The suggestion appears as one line under the card ("jordan_w: same as “…”"); the owner or author can **Combine** or **Dismiss** it, and the suggester can **Withdraw** it. On the open-weights board, jordan_w has suggested combining “Nobody abroad will trust a Chinese AI service…” with the trust argument.
  - People who can only view or vote don't see the handle.
- Each card has a **⋯** menu. On your own versions: **Edit wording and context** (in place; like Reddit, any edit is allowed and the byline then says "edited", which shows the earlier wording; importance and articulation votes stay, but credibility assessments start over, and the form says how many before you save), **Combine with another argument**, **Make it a separate argument**, and **Delete**. On other people's versions the menu says only they can edit it and offers **Suggest a revised version**, which opens a new version in the same row starting from that wording; the board owner also gets combine and separate, and other contributors get **Suggest combining**.
- Options are editable too. The **⋯** on an option's header renames it in place (Enter saves, Escape keeps the old name) or deletes it, which is allowed only when it has no arguments and the question keeps at least two options; Undo is available. The dashed **+ Option** after the last column adds an option (up to four): name it and press Enter; leaving it unnamed removes it.
- In an expanded card, **Add a source** opens a short form: the source, where to find it, and what it shows; **Backs up & limits** adds two optional fields. Sources you added carry a "You" tag and can be edited or removed (with Undo). Anyone can choose **Can't open it?** to report a source; it is marked Unavailable on every version that cites it, and "Yes" needs at least one available source. Changing a version's evidence starts its credibility assessments over, because they judged other evidence: the source form warns first ("8 assessments start over when you save."), **Remove** asks for a second click ("Remove · 8 assessments start over"), and afterwards the evidence section says "The evidence changed, so assessments started over." Editing a shared source resets every version that cites it. Reporting a source unavailable doesn't reset anything.
- Every question, option, and version has a speech-bubble icon with its open-comment count. Click it to open the comments panel next to that item; the item gets a dark ring. Comment, reply, resolve or reopen a thread, and delete your own comments (⌘/Ctrl + Enter also posts). Resolved threads fold under "Resolved". On wide screens the board makes room for the panel; on narrower ones it floats on the side away from the item, or just below it.
- Under the question: the author, the date, and who can see it ("POD 6 only", "Private", or "Anyone with link"; the space is listed separately when the question isn't limited to it). The cog opens settings beside the board: rename the question and choose its space; who can see, vote, and contribute is under **Share**. **Reset to the original example** (click twice) restores every example board.

**Analytics.** The Product Page and this page send usage to PostHog (`../analytics.js`): page views, clicks, session replays, and the name of each prototype action (for example `prototype: unfold`), with board and card ids but never anything typed. A first-visit notice (bottom left, on the Product Page and here) says so, with **OK** and **Turn off**; the choice is kept in this browser (`steelman-analytics` in localStorage) and can be changed on the [privacy page](../privacy.html) or in Account. Browsers that send Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track send nothing and see no notice. Local runs are tagged `environment: local`. Replays can see inside the prototype because its frame now shares the page's origin (`allow-same-origin`).

Everything a contributor can edit (the question, options, arguments, versions, and sources) is saved as one document in the browser, so later edits can change any part of it. The claims, sources, and votes are illustrative. Interactions are local to the prototype; there is no shared backend.
